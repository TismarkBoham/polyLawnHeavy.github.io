Validation — how do we know this is accurate?
This is a real, re-runnable test against a specific published finding — not a claimed accuracy number. It fetches live climate and soil data for the University of Minnesota's turfgrass research site in St. Paul, MN, scores all species under low- and high-input management using this tool's own engine, and checks the result against Braun & Watkins et al. (2016, HortScience), a field trial run at that exact site that measured how turfgrass species actually performed across mowing and nitrogen regimes.

The benchmark above is a spot-check; this tool is the real thing. UMN's Low Input Turf program (lowinputturf.umn.edu) and NTEP-DB are the actual gold-standard ground-truth source. NTEP-DB isn't a public API, so nothing here pulls it in automatically — but once you have composition data in hand (from NTEP-DB exports, published trial tables, or your own field surveys), paste it above, one site at a time, to see how this tool's predictions hold up. Run it across several sites and years and the pattern in the correlation numbers — not any single run — is what should drive recalibrating the plant tolerance ranges.
